# UNIVERSAL AI IMAGE-TO-VIDEO MASTER PROMPT

## Advanced Photorealistic Video Generation System

### Beyond the Venue | Universal Creative Production Standard

## PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
Convert the uploaded reference photograph into a realistic, high-quality, cinematic video using AI image-to-video generation.

This is NOT a photo editor, slideshow creator, image transition generator or simple camera-animation task.

The objective is to transform a static photograph into a believable moving scene in which the actual subjects, objects, environmental elements and surroundings demonstrate appropriate physical movement.

The generated result should resemble footage captured by a professional cinematographer using a real camera in the actual environment.

The instructions below apply to ALL possible image categories, including but not limited to:

- Weddings, engagements, receptions and celebrations.
- Event decorations, stages, floral arrangements and venues.
- Birthdays, anniversaries and private parties.
- People, portraits, fashion, lifestyle and social gatherings.
- Animals, birds, wildlife and nature.
- Landscapes, mountains, beaches, rivers and waterfalls.
- Buildings, architecture, interiors and real estate.
- Restaurants, hotels, cafes and hospitality spaces.
- Products, commercial photography and advertisements.
- Cars, bikes, transportation and machinery.
- Food, beverages and culinary photography.
- Travel, tourism, cities and streets.
- Art, crafts, jewellery and creative installations.
- Nature, weather, sky and environmental scenes.
- Any other photograph uploaded by the user.
Do not assume the category in advance. Analyse every image independently.

---

# 1. INTELLIGENT IMAGE UNDERSTANDING
Before generating the video, thoroughly interpret the uploaded photograph.

Identify:

1. The primary subject.
2. Secondary subjects.
3. Background elements.
4. Foreground elements.
5. Objects capable of natural movement.
6. Environmental conditions.
7. Lighting conditions.
8. Spatial relationships and depth.
9. The scene's likely context and purpose.
10. Possible natural interactions between subjects and objects.
Determine what is happening in the image and what could realistically happen immediately before or after the captured moment.

Do not introduce a fictional storyline that contradicts the photograph.

The animation must be context-aware and specific to the image.

# 2. ACTUAL OBJECT AND SUBJECT MOVEMENT
The most important requirement is genuine movement within the scene.

Animate the actual visible subjects and objects wherever appropriate.

Depending on the image category, consider the following possibilities.

### A. Human movement
When people are present, generate natural movements such as:

- Walking forward or backwards.
- Turning their heads.
- Looking around.
- Smiling and changing facial expressions subtly.
- Talking and gesturing naturally.
- Moving their hands and arms.
- Sitting down or standing up.
- Adjusting clothing.
- Interacting with nearby people.
- Handling objects.
- Performing context-appropriate activities.
- Natural breathing and subtle body movement.
- Appropriate group interactions.
Maintain facial identity, body proportions, clothing, hairstyles and other identifiable visual characteristics.

Avoid exaggerated gestures, unnatural expressions and distorted anatomy.

Do not invent additional people.

### B. Animals and wildlife
When animals are present, consider:

- Walking, running or jumping.
- Head and eye movements.
- Ear and tail movement.
- Wing flapping.
- Flying or swimming.
- Natural interaction with the environment.
- Feeding or grooming behaviour where appropriate.
Preserve species characteristics, body structure and natural behaviour.

### C. Nature and environmental movement
When natural elements are visible, animate:

- Trees and leaves swaying.
- Flowers moving gently.
- Grass responding to wind.
- Clouds travelling naturally.
- Water flowing.
- Ocean waves moving and breaking.
- Waterfalls cascading.
- Rain falling.
- Snow falling.
- Smoke moving with airflow.
- Dust and atmospheric particles.
- Sunlight and shadows changing subtly.
- Reflections moving naturally.
Respect the actual environmental conditions in the photograph.

### D. Fabric and decorative elements
When fabric or decorations are present, animate:

- Curtains swaying.
- Drapes flowing.
- Clothing moving naturally.
- Ribbons fluttering.
- Hanging decorations swinging.
- Floral installations moving gently.
- Loose petals falling.
- Decorative tassels responding to air movement.
- Balloons moving subtly.
- Hanging lights swinging slightly.
Preserve the original arrangement, colour scheme and decorative design.

### E. Lighting and illumination
Where appropriate, generate:

- Candle flames flickering.
- Fairy lights shimmering.
- Decorative lights glowing.
- Neon lights illuminating the surroundings.
- Natural reflections.
- Realistic light interaction.
- Subtle changes in illumination.
- Moving shadows caused by actual scene movement.
- Light rays and atmospheric illumination.
Avoid excessive flashing, artificial brightness and inconsistent lighting.

### F. Water, liquids and fluids
When water or liquids are present, consider:

- Flowing water.
- Ripples.
- Splashes.
- Droplets falling.
- Pouring liquids.
- Fountains operating.
- Waves moving.
- Reflections responding to water movement.
- Natural fluid interactions.
Maintain realistic fluid behaviour.

### G. Vehicles and machinery
When vehicles or machines are visible, consider:

- Wheels rotating.
- Vehicles moving.
- Headlights illuminating the surroundings.
- Moving mechanical components.
- Realistic operational movement.
- Smoke or exhaust where contextually appropriate.
- Natural interactions between vehicles and the environment.
Never create impossible mechanical movements.

### H. Food and products
When food or products are present, consider:

- Steam rising from hot food.
- Liquids being poured.
- Ingredients moving naturally.
- Packaging being opened.
- Product rotation where appropriate.
- Reflections changing with lighting.
- Realistic interactions with hands when people are visible.
Preserve product identity, branding, packaging, labels and text.

Do not arbitrarily alter the product.

### I. Architecture and interiors
When buildings or interiors are present, consider:

- Moving curtains.
- Changing natural light.
- Moving shadows.
- Subtle environmental movement.
- Operating fans or visible mechanical elements.
- Reflections on glass.
- Natural movement of plants.
- Appropriate movement of people already present.
Do not change the architectural structure, room dimensions or furniture placement.

These are context-specific possibilities, not mandatory effects. Only animate elements actually supported by the photograph.

---

# 3. PHYSICAL REALISM AND MOTION ACCURACY
Every movement must obey realistic physical behaviour.

Enforce:

- Natural acceleration and deceleration.
- Realistic gravity.
- Correct object weight and inertia.
- Appropriate movement speed.
- Consistent object dimensions.
- Natural collision and contact behaviour.
- Realistic fabric deformation.
- Accurate water and fluid behaviour.
- Anatomically plausible human and animal movement.
- Consistent object positioning and spatial relationships.
Avoid:

- Floating objects without a physical explanation.
- Objects moving through solid surfaces.
- Objects changing shape.
- Objects appearing and disappearing.
- Sudden direction changes.
- Random movements.
- Excessive movement.
- Unnatural physics.
Motion must be subtle when the scene requires subtlety and dynamic when the scene supports dynamic movement.

---

# 4. CAMERA MOVEMENT AND CINEMATOGRAPHY
Do not rely on camera movement alone to create the video.

Actual subject and object movement is the priority.

Choose an appropriate professional camera technique according to the scene.

Possible camera movements:

- Cinematic push-in.
- Cinematic pull-back.
- Slow tracking shot.
- Smooth horizontal pan.
- Controlled vertical tilt.
- Dolly movement.
- Orbital camera movement.
- Sideways tracking.
- Parallax movement.
- Perspective-based camera movement.
- Controlled handheld movement.
- Static camera with moving subjects.
- Subtle rack focus.
- Foreground-to-background focus transition.
Use only movements that can be achieved without compromising the original scene.

Avoid unnecessary camera rotation, excessive zooming, unstable motion or artificial perspective distortion.

# 5. DEPTH, PERSPECTIVE AND SPATIAL CONSISTENCY
Generate realistic depth wherever possible.

- Maintain correct foreground, middle-ground and background relationships.
- Preserve object boundaries.
- Maintain realistic perspective.
- Avoid stretching or warping.
- Avoid background displacement.
- Preserve the original composition.
- Generate natural depth-of-field when suitable.
- Maintain consistent subject positioning throughout the clip.
Do not invent hidden objects or details that cannot be reasonably inferred.

# 6. SUBJECT AND IMAGE PRESERVATION
The uploaded photograph is the primary visual reference and must remain the source of truth.

Preserve:

- Original people and their identities.
- Faces and facial characteristics.
- Clothing and accessories.
- Products and their designs.
- Furniture and architecture.
- Decoration arrangements.
- Original colours.
- Background elements.
- Lighting direction.
- Scene composition.
- Recognisable environmental details.
- Text, logos and brand elements.
Do not unnecessarily redesign, replace, remove, recolour or duplicate existing objects.

Do not create additional subjects unless explicitly requested.

Do not change the scene's purpose or identity.

# 7. REALISTIC HUMAN IDENTITY AND ANATOMY
When people are present, pay special attention to identity preservation.

- Maintain consistent faces.
- Preserve facial proportions.
- Maintain realistic skin texture.
- Preserve clothing and accessories.
- Keep the correct number of people.
- Maintain anatomically correct hands.
- Avoid extra fingers or missing limbs.
- Avoid unnatural eye movement.
- Avoid distorted facial expressions.
- Avoid identity switching.
- Avoid changes in age or appearance.
All movement must look physically natural.

# 8. LIGHTING, COLOUR AND VISUAL CONSISTENCY
Maintain consistent lighting throughout the video.

Use:

- Realistic highlights.
- Natural shadows.
- Accurate reflections.
- Consistent exposure.
- Appropriate colour temperature.
- Realistic ambient illumination.
- Natural contrast.
- Accurate surface textures.
Adapt the visual treatment to the original photograph.

Do not automatically apply warm, golden, cinematic or vintage filters to every image.

Do not oversaturate colours or introduce artificial lighting effects.

# 9. CINEMATIC VISUAL STYLE
Aim for professional commercial-quality cinematography.

The final video should have:

- Photorealistic rendering.
- Natural motion.
- High-quality textures.
- Accurate details.
- Smooth movement.
- Realistic depth.
- Stable geometry.
- Natural environmental interactions.
- Professional lighting.
- Consistent visual identity.
Avoid cartoon-like animation, excessive CGI effects, artificial rendering and obvious AI-generated distortions unless specifically requested.

# 10. TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
Maintain continuity across every frame.

Ensure:

- The primary subject remains recognisable.
- Objects maintain consistent shapes.
- Background structures remain stable.
- Lighting does not change randomly.
- Movement evolves continuously.
- No flickering geometry.
- No sudden object duplication.
- No disappearing elements.
- No abrupt subject transformations.
- No unexpected scene transitions.
Generate a coherent continuous scene rather than a collection of unrelated frames.

# 11. VIDEO DURATION AND MOTION INTENSITY
Use the duration supported by the selected generation model.

Prioritise meaningful movement over unnecessary clip length.

Movement intensity should be determined by the subject and environment.

Use:

- Subtle movement for portraits, interiors and delicate decorations.
- Moderate movement for lifestyle, travel and environmental footage.
- Dynamic movement for sports, vehicles, animals and action scenes when appropriate.
Do not force dramatic motion into a photograph that requires a calm atmosphere.

# 12. AUDIO REQUIREMENTS
Do not automatically add background music, songs, voiceovers or soundtracks.

If the generation model supports ambient sound and the scene benefits from it, only use realistic environmental audio when explicitly requested.

Otherwise, generate a silent video.

# 13. STRICT NEGATIVE INSTRUCTIONS
The following outcomes are unacceptable:

- Static photographs with only camera zoom.
- Simple panning across a still image.
- Slideshow transitions.
- Frozen subjects.
- Fake movement created entirely by motion blur.
- Unnatural object animation.
- Distorted human faces.
- Deformed hands and bodies.
- Duplicated objects.
- Changing product designs.
- Changing architectural structures.
- Inconsistent backgrounds.
- Random environmental effects.
- Unnecessary additional subjects.
- Unwanted text or watermarks.
- Unnatural lighting changes.
- Excessive camera shake.
- Melting, warping or morphing objects.
- Abrupt scene changes.
- Artificial cartoon effects.
- Visually implausible physical interactions.

# 14. FINAL QUALITY CONTROL
Before finalising the generated result, evaluate:

1. Is there actual movement within the scene?
2. Are the movements appropriate for the uploaded photograph?
3. Do the subjects and objects retain their original appearance?
4. Is the movement physically believable?
5. Are faces and body structures consistent?
6. Is the background stable?
7. Is the lighting consistent?
8. Is the camera movement appropriate?
9. Does the result look like real captured footage?
10. Are there obvious AI-generated distortions?
If the result fails these checks, refine the generation using a more conservative motion instruction and regenerate where possible.

# 15. FINAL GENERATION DIRECTIVE
Generate an actual AI-created video from the uploaded image.

Do not produce an edited photograph, animated still, slideshow, transition effect or simple zoom sequence.

The video must contain genuine, contextually appropriate movement of visible subjects, objects and environmental elements wherever the photograph supports it.

Use intelligent scene understanding to determine what should move, how it should move, and how the camera should capture that movement.

Preserve the original photograph's identity while transforming its static scene into believable moving footage.

**THE FINAL STANDARD: THE RESULT SHOULD LOOK AS THOUGH THE ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH HAS COME TO LIFE AND A PROFESSIONAL VIDEOGRAPHER HAS CAPTURED THE SCENE IN REAL TIME.**
